# Vowel harmony

Almost every Kazakh ending has two shapes: one with a **back** vowel and one with a **front** vowel. The plural is **-лар** or **-лер**; “in” is **-да** or **-де**. Which one you use depends on the word's **last vowel**.

| Back vowels | Front vowels |
|---|---|
| а, о, ұ, ы | ә, ө, ү, і, е |
| endings with **а** or **ы** | endings with **е** or **і** |

So:

- бала (child), last vowel а → балалар, балада
- үй (house), last vowel ү → үйлер, үйде
- кітап (book), last vowel а → кітаптар, кітапта
- мектеп (school), last vowel е → мектептер, мектепте

Native Kazakh words usually have only back or only front vowels (бала, қала, үйлер, мектеп). Words borrowed from Arabic or Persian can mix them, like кітап, but the ending still follows the **last** vowel.

::forms plural бала қала тау үй мектеп кітап

## Two tricky letters

- **у** and **и** can belong to either family. After a consonant, **у** usually counts as back (су → сулар, минут → минуттар) and **и** as front (би → билер). After a vowel they are just consonant sounds, and the vowel before them decides: күйеу (husband) → күйеулер.
- Some **Russian loanwords** are pronounced with front vowels whatever their spelling: банк → банкте, банктер. Körti lists these one by one.

## The other half: the last sound

Harmony decides the *vowel* of the ending. The *consonant* it starts with depends on the sound the word ends in: a vowel, л, м/н/ң, з/ж, or a voiceless consonant like п, т, к, қ, с. You'll see this in every topic that follows, starting with [plurals](learn:plural).
