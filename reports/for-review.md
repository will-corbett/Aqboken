# Aqböken: for review by a Kazakh speaker

Aqböken (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/) teaches Kazakh to English speakers. Its word forms are made by rule and checked against Wiktionary, but everything listed here was written by us and hasn’t been checked by a native speaker. Please mark anything that is wrong, unnatural, or not how people say it, and give the better version if you can. Comments can refer to item numbers (W12, L3, D40 …).

What matters most: **is the Kazakh correct and natural?** Is the English meaning right? Would a teacher explain it this way?

## 1. Words and their meanings

Our word lists, by theme. Please check the spelling, the meaning, and whether it’s the word people actually use (and not old-fashioned, regional or overly formal).

## 2. Kazakh in the lessons

Every line of the Learn pages that contains Kazakh, with its English. Tables of forms are made by the rules and not listed (they are checked against Wiktionary).

### The alphabet (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/alphabet/)

- **L1** Letters marked * appear only in words borrowed from Russian (цирк, чемпион, юрист) and have no Latin letter of their own; Aqböken writes their sounds.
- **L2** | Ә ә | Ä ä | *a* in “cat” | әке (father) |
- **L3** | Ғ ғ | Ğ ğ | a soft, throaty *g*, like French *r* | ағаш (tree) |
- **L4** | Қ қ | Q q | a *k* made far back in the throat | қала (city) |
- **L5** | Ң ң | Ñ ñ | *ng* in “sing” | жаңа (new) |
- **L6** | Ө ө | Ö ö | German *ö*: say *e* with rounded lips | өзен (river) |
- **L7** | Ұ ұ | Ū ū | *u* in “put” | ұл (son) |
- **L8** | Ү ү | Ü ü | German *ü*: say *i* with rounded lips | үй (house) |
- **L9** | Һ һ | H h | *h* (rare, in a few loanwords) | гауһар (diamond) |
- **L10** | І і | I ı | a short *i*, as in “bit” | іні (younger brother) |
- **L11** - Ы ы (Latin *Y y*) is a short vowel made at the back of the mouth, a bit like the *e* in “roses”: қыз (girl).
- **L12** - У у is *u* after a consonant but *w* after a vowel: тау (mountain) sounds like *taw*.
- **L13** - И и is *i* or *iy*; Й й is *y* as in “yes”. In Latin both are written *İ i*.
- **L14** - Е е at the start of a word sounds *ye*: ертең (tomorrow).
- **L15** The vowels split into back vowels (а, о, ұ, ы) and front vowels (ә, ө, ү, і, е). This matters for every ending in the language: that's vowel harmony, the next topic.

### The seven cases (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/cases/)

- **L16** Where English uses little words like *to*, *in* and *from*, Kazakh adds an ending. There are seven cases (септіктер); one is just the plain word.
- **L17** | Nominative (Атау) | – | the plain form | мектеп |
- **L18** | Genitive (Ілік) | -ның/-дың/-тың | of, ’s | мектептің (of the school) |
- **L19** | Dative (Барыс) | -ға/-қа | to, towards | мектепке (to school) |
- **L20** | Accusative (Табыс) | -ны/-ды/-ты | the (definite object) | мектепті (the school) |
- **L21** | Locative (Жатыс) | -да/-та | in, at, on | мектепте (at school) |
- **L22** | Ablative (Шығыс) | -дан/-нан/-тан | from | мектептен (from school) |
- **L23** | Instrumental (Көмектес) | -мен/-бен/-пен | with, by | автобуспен (by bus) |
- **L24** Each ending has front and back forms (-ға/-ге) and changes its first consonant to suit the word's last sound, just like the plural.
- **L25** - Dative for where you're going: Мен мектепке барамын. (I'm going to school.)
- **L26** - Locative for where something is: Ол үйде. (He's at home.)
- **L27** - Ablative for where you're from: Мен Алматыдан келдім. (I came from Almaty.)
- **L28** - Instrumental for how, or with whom: Біз автобуспен барамыз. (We go by bus.) Досыммен (with my friend).
- **L29** - Accusative only for a *particular* thing: Мен кітап оқимын (I read books / a book) but Мен кітапты оқимын (I'm reading *the* book).
- **L30** - Genitive for an owner, with the possessive on the thing owned: баланың кітабы (the child's book). See My, your, his.
- **L31** The genitive is -ның after vowels and м/н/ң, -дың after other voiced sounds, -тың after voiceless ones. The accusative is -ны after vowels, -ды after voiced consonants, -ты after voiceless ones. The instrumental is always -мен except after з/ж (-бен) and voiceless consonants (-пен); it never changes for harmony.

### Right now (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/continuous/)

- **L32** The present tense (барамын, оқимын) is for habits and plans. For what's happening at this moment, Kazakh uses a verb form ending in -ып followed by жатыр (literally “lying”), which takes the personal ending:
- **L33** - Мен кітап оқып жатырмын. I'm reading a book (right now).
- **L34** - Ол хат жазып жатыр. He's writing a letter.
- **L35** - Біз шай ішіп жатырмыз. We're having tea.
- **L36** - After a vowel, just -п: оқы- → оқып, ойна- → ойнап, сөйле- → сөйлеп.
- **L37** - After a consonant, -ып or -іп by harmony: бар- → барып, кел- → келіп. A final қ/к becomes ғ/г: шық- → шығып.
- **L38** - A final п after a vowel becomes у: тап- → тауып, жап- → жауып.
- **L39** бару and келу use the -а/-е form instead: Мен үйге бара жатырмын (I'm going home). Ол келе жатыр (He's coming).
- **L40** Instead of жатыр, Kazakh often uses the verb that matches the position: отыр (sitting), тұр (standing) or жүр (going about, these days). Ол кітап оқып отыр: he's sitting reading a book. You'll understand these once you know жатыр; the drill practises жатыр.

### “I am …” (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/copula/)

- **L41** - Мен мұғаліммін. I am a teacher.
- **L42** - Сен студентсің. You are a student.
- **L43** - Ол дәрігер. He (she) is a doctor.
- **L44** - Біз қазақпыз. We are Kazakhs.
- **L45** - With мен (I) and біз (we), the ending's first consonant follows the word's last sound: -мын after vowels and most voiced sounds, -бын after з/ж, -пын after voiceless consonants (студентпін, қазақпын).
- **L46** - With сен, сіз, сендер, сіздер the ending always starts with с.
- **L47** - With ол (he, she) and олар (they) there's no ending at all.
- **L48** Kazakh has two “you”s. Сен is for a friend, a child or family; сіз is polite, for someone older or someone you don't know. In the plural: сендер and сіздер. Use сіз when in doubt.
- **L49** Put емес (not) after the word, and the personal ending goes on емес: Мен студент емеспін. (I'm not a student.)

### Going to (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/future/)

- **L50** The present tense already covers the future: Мен ертең барамын (I'll go tomorrow). To say what you plan or intend to do, add -мақ to the stem, then the “I am” ending:
- **L51** - Мен ертең кітапханаға бармақпын. I'm going to go to the library tomorrow.
- **L52** - Біз кино көрмекпіз. We're going to watch a film.
- **L53** -мақ/-мек after vowels and р, й, у, л; -бақ/-бек after м, н, ң, з, ж; -пақ/-пек after voiceless consonants (айтпақ, кетпек). It's the same consonant rule as the question particle and the negative.
- **L54** Then the personal ending, which after қ/к starts with п: бармақпын, бармақсың, бармақ, бармақпыз.
- **L55** Add емес with the ending: Мен бармақ емеспін. (I'm not going to go.)

### Vowel harmony (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/harmony/)

- **L56** Almost every Kazakh ending has two shapes: one with a back vowel and one with a front vowel. The plural is -лар or -лер; “in” is -да or -де. Which one you use depends on the word's last vowel.
- **L57** | а, о, ұ, ы | ә, ө, ү, і, е |
- **L58** | endings with а or ы | endings with е or і |
- **L59** - бала (child), last vowel а → балалар, балада
- **L60** - үй (house), last vowel ү → үйлер, үйде
- **L61** - кітап (book), last vowel а → кітаптар, кітапта
- **L62** - мектеп (school), last vowel е → мектептер, мектепте
- **L63** Native Kazakh words usually have only back or only front vowels (бала, қала, үйлер, мектеп). Words borrowed from Arabic or Persian can mix them, like кітап, but the ending still follows the last vowel.
- **L64** - у and и can belong to either family. After a consonant, у usually counts as back (су → сулар, минут → минуттар) and и as front (би → билер). After a vowel they are just consonant sounds, and the vowel before them decides: күйеу (husband) → күйеулер.
- **L65** - Some Russian loanwords are pronounced with front vowels whatever their spelling: банк → банкте, банктер. Aqböken lists these one by one.
- **L66** Harmony decides the *vowel* of the ending. The *consonant* it starts with depends on the sound the word ends in: a vowel, л, м/н/ң, з/ж, or a voiceless consonant like п, т, к, қ, с. You'll see this in every topic that follows, starting with plurals.

### Requests and commands (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/imperative/)

- **L67** The bare stem is a command to a friend: Бар! (Go!), Кел! (Come!), Оқы! (Read!). Other endings make it polite, plural, or a suggestion.
- **L68** - To a stranger, someone older, a customer: the polite -(ы)ңыз: Кіріңіз! (Come in!) Отырыңыз! (Sit down!)
- **L69** - To several people: -(ы)ңдар, or the polite -(ы)ңыздар.
- **L70** - “Let's …”: -айық/-ейік: Барайық! (Let's go!)
- **L71** - “Let me …”: -айын/-ейін: Айтайын. (Let me tell you.)
- **L72** - “Let him/her …”: -сын/-сін: Келсін. (Let him come.)
- **L73** Put the negative -ма/-ме (or -ба/-бе, -па/-пе) on the stem: Барма! (Don't go!) Айтпаңыз! (Please don't say it.)

### Numbers (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/numbers/)

- **L74** Kazakh numbers are very regular: learn 1–10, the tens, жүз (100) and мың (1,000), and you can say any number.
- **L75** - 100 is just жүз, and 1,000 is мың (or бір мың).
- **L76** - A noun after a number stays singular: үш кітап (three books).

### Past tense (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/past/)

- **L77** The everyday past tense is -ды/-ді (or -ты/-ті after voiceless consonants) plus a short personal ending: *бардым* is “I went”.
- **L78** - мен: -м (бардым), сен: -ң (бардың), сіз: -ңыз (бардыңыз)
- **L79** - ол: nothing (барды)
- **L80** - біз: -қ/-к (бардық, келдік)
- **L81** - сендер: -ңдар (бардыңдар), сіздер: -ңыздар (бардыңыздар)
- **L82** The consonant: -ды after vowels and voiced consonants (бардым, оқыдым), -ты after voiceless ones (айттым, кеттім).
- **L83** -ма/-ба/-па goes after the stem, and the past ending after it is always -ды/-ді:

### Plurals (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/plural/)

- **L84** The plural ending is -лар/-лер, and it has six shapes. The vowel follows vowel harmony; the first consonant follows the word's last sound.
- **L85** - After a number: екі кітап (two books), бес бала (five children). The number already says there's more than one.
- **L86** - After көп (many) and бірнеше (several): көп адам (many people).
- **L87** Kazakh piles endings up, always in the same order: word + plural + possessive + case. So “in the books” is кітап + тар + да = кітаптарда. Each ending follows the sounds of the one before it.

### In my house, to your friend (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/posscases/)

- **L88** Endings stack: first the possessive, then the case. “In my house” is үй + ім + де = үйімде.
- **L89** - After -м (my) and -ң (your), the dative is just -а/-е: кітабыма (to my book), досыңа (to your friend).
- **L90** - After the 3rd-person -ы/-сы (his/her), the cases take н: кітабына, кітабында, кітабынан, кітабын.
- **L91** - Otherwise the ending follows the possessive's last sound like any other word: кітабымда, кітабымнан, кітабымызға.
- **L92** - Менің үйімде. In my house.
- **L93** - Досыма хат жаздым. I wrote a letter to my friend.
- **L94** - Біз ауылымызға барамыз. We're going to our village.

### My, your, his (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/possessive/)

- **L95** - менің кітабым: my book
- **L96** - сенің кітабың: your book (friend)
- **L97** - сіздің кітабыңыз: your book (polite)
- **L98** - оның кітабы: his/her book
- **L99** - біздің кітабымыз: our book
- **L100** After a vowel the ending is short (балам, әкесі); after a consonant it starts with a vowel (үйім, досы). Harmony picks ы or і, as always.
- **L101** - Before a vowel, a final п, к, қ becomes б, г, ғ: кітап → кітабым, жүрек → жүрегім, құлақ → құлағым.
- **L102** - A few nouns drop a vowel: ауыз (mouth) → аузым, мұрын (nose) → мұрным.
- **L103** - Some loanwords don't change: банк → банкі.
- **L104** “Asqar's book” is Асқардың кітабы: the owner takes the genitive (-дың), and the thing owned takes the his/her ending (-ы). The same pattern names many things: Қазақстанның астанасы (the capital of Kazakhstan).

### Postpositions (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/postpositions/)

- **L105** - Сабақтан кейін. After the lesson.
- **L106** - Үйге дейін. As far as the house.
- **L107** - Досыммен бірге. Together with my friend.
- **L108** - Мен үшін. For me.
- **L109** - Қазақстан туралы. About Kazakhstan.
- **L110** Learn each postposition with its case, as a pair: -ға дейін, -дан кейін, -мен бірге. Most take the plain form (үшін, туралы, арқылы, сияқты); the rest take the dative, ablative or instrumental.

### Present tense (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/present/)

- **L111** The Kazakh “present” is really a present-future: барамын means *I go*, *I'm going* (in general) and *I will go*. Words like ертең (tomorrow) make the future clear: Мен ертең барамын.
- **L112** Take the verb's stem (the dictionary form without -у: бару → бар-), add -а/-е (or -й after a vowel), then the personal ending.
- **L113** - After a consonant: барамын, келемін. A final п/қ/к becomes б/ғ/г: тап- → табамын, шық- → шығамын.
- **L114** - After а, е and other vowels: тыңдаймын, сөйлеймін.
- **L115** - After ы/і, the vowel and the й merge into и: оқы- → оқимын, оқиды.
- **L116** - The ол form ends in -ды/-ді: барады, оқиды.
- **L117** Put -ма/-ме (or -ба/-бе, -па/-пе, by the stem's last sound) between the stem and the ending, then й:
- **L118** For “I'm reading (at this moment)”, Kazakh adds a helper verb: Мен кітап оқып отырмын. Aqböken's drills practise the simple present first.

### Pronouns in the cases (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/pronouns/)

- **L119** The pronouns take the same seven cases as nouns, but мен, сен and ол have some forms you just need to learn.
- **L120** - маған, саған, оған: the dative (to me, to you, to him/her) changes the vowel. Маған кітап бер. (Give me a book.)
- **L121** - ол builds most of its forms on он-: оның, оны, онда, онымен; but the ablative is одан.
- **L122** - менімен, сенімен: “with me, with you” are built on the genitive.
- **L123** сіз, біз and олар are regular: they take the same endings as nouns (сізге, бізде, олардан).

### Yes/no questions (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/questions/)

- **L124** To turn a statement into a yes/no question, add the particle ма at the end. It's written as a separate word, but it follows the last word's sounds like an ending:
- **L125** - Сен барасың ба? Are you going?
- **L126** - Сіз түсінесіз бе? Do you understand?
- **L127** - Ол келді ме? Did he come?
- **L128** Иә (yes) or жоқ (no). For “not”, use the negative verb (Жоқ, бармаймын) or емес with nouns (Жоқ, бұл кітап емес).
- **L129** With a question word (кім, не, қайда, қашан, қалай), don't add ма: Сен қайда барасың? (Where are you going?)

### Want to (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/learn/want/)

- **L130** “I want to go” is literally “my going comes”: the verb takes -ғы and a possessive ending, followed by келеді:
- **L131** - Менің барғым келеді. I want to go.
- **L132** - Сенің шай ішкің келе ме? Do you want some tea?
- **L133** - Оның ұйықтағысы келеді. He wants to sleep.
- **L134** 1. The stem: бар-, іш-, оқы-.
- **L135** 2. -ғы/-гі after vowels and voiced sounds, -қы/-кі after voiceless ones: барғы-, ішкі-, айтқы-.
- **L136** 3. The possessive for the person: -м (my), -ң (your), -ңыз (your, polite), -сы (his/her), -мыз (our).
- **L137** 4. келеді, which stays the same for every person.
- **L138** Because the person is shown by a possessive, the pronoun before it is in the genitive: менің, сенің, оның.
- **L139** Make келеді negative: Менің барғым келмейді. (I don't want to go.)

### The seven cases (https://will-corbett.github.io/Aqboken/drill/cases/)

- **D25** Genitive (Ілік): of, ’s: *мезгіл* (season; time); hint “of the season” → **мезгілдің**. Genitive (of, ’s): мезгіл ends in л, and its last vowel, і, is a front vowel (so е/і in the ending). So -дің: мезгілдің.
- **D26** Dative (Барыс): to, towards, for: *мұғалім* (teacher); hint “to the teacher” → **мұғалімге**. Dative (to, towards, for): мұғалім ends in м, a nasal (м, н, ң), and its last vowel, і, is a front vowel (so е/і in the ending). So -ге: мұғалімге.
- **D27** Locative (Жатыс): in, at, on: *ас үй* (kitchen); hint “in / at the kitchen” → **ас үйде**. Locative (in, at, on): ас үй ends in й, a sonorant (р, й, у, и), and its last vowel, ү, is a front vowel (so е/і in the ending). So -де: ас үйде.

- **D29** Instrumental (Көмектес): with, by: *чек* (receipt); hint “with / by the receipt” → **чекпен**. Instrumental (with, by): чек ends in к, a voiceless consonant, and the instrumental ending never changes its vowel. So -пен: чекпен.
- **D30** Ablative (Шығыс): from, out of: *белдемше* (skirt); hint “from the skirt” → **белдемшеден**. Ablative (from, out of): белдемше ends in е, a vowel, and its last vowel, е, is a front vowel (so е/і in the ending). So -ден: белдемшеден.

## 6. Words Wiktionary doesn’t have

We kept these because we believe they’re right; please confirm.

- **қай**: a basic question word (which?) with no English Wiktionary entry yet; it never changes form.
- **ас үй**: kitchen, written as two words (ас “food” + үй “house”); үй is in Wiktionary, ас only inside phrases. Its endings follow үй (ас үйде), as the rules give.
- **қайту**: a basic verb (to return) with no Wiktionary entry yet; regular: stem қайт-, like айту (whose table matches).
- **жуу**: a basic verb (to wash) with no Wiktionary entry yet; stem жу-, present жуамын like other stems in -у.
- **сөйлесу**: a basic verb (to talk with) with no Wiktionary entry yet; stem сөйлес-, regular.
- **пісіру**: a basic verb (to cook) with no Wiktionary entry yet; stem пісір-, regular.
- **ауыру**: a basic verb (to hurt, to be ill: басым ауырады) with no Wiktionary entry yet; stem ауыр-, regular.
- **сатып алу**: to buy: сатып (the -ып form of сату, sell) + алу (take), the everyday verb; алу is in Wiktionary.
- **әкелу**: a basic verb (to bring) with no Wiktionary entry yet; stem әкел-, regular.
- **қоңырау шалу**: to phone: қоңырау (bell) + шалу (ring); қоңырау is in Wiktionary, шалу not yet.
